# The BVC Research Method

## Master Research & Writing Framework

**Beyond Vibe Coding · Computer Science Research · Dong Hong**

> **Research deeply. Explain simply. Think like an engineer.**

This is the governing method for the Computer Science Research track. It is flexible, not a rigid article template.

## 1. Purpose

Produce work that is technically deep, scientifically grounded, engineer-oriented, accessible to beginners, minimal, experience-aware, and intellectually honest. The goal is to help readers understand a mechanism, reason about it, test it where possible, and make better engineering decisions.

## 2. Research Philosophy

1. **Understand before using.** Start with the problem and mechanism, not a tool tutorial.
2. **Mechanism over memorization.** Explain causal relationships, data flow, complexity, and behavior.
3. **Intuition before formalism.** Build a mental model, then introduce precise terminology, math, and implementation details.
4. **Evidence before claims.** Use primary papers, official specifications, architecture manuals, textbooks, source code, or reproducible experiments.
5. **Engineering is about decisions.** Explain where the concept matters, its constraints, and trade-offs.
6. **Human ownership.** AI may assist research and drafting; people remain responsible for verification, reasoning, decisions, and conclusions.

## 3. The Nine-Part Research Formula

Use this as the default conceptual progression. Reorder, combine, or omit parts when the topic demands it.

### 01 — The Question
Open with a precise question, misconception, surprising observation, or engineering pain point. Avoid clickbait.

### 02 — The Intuition
Explain the idea with a relatable scenario, analogy, small example, or visual. State where an analogy stops matching reality.

### 03 — The Mechanism
Explain what happens inside the system: data flow, algorithm steps, state transitions, hardware behavior, or protocol interactions.

### 04 — The Deep Dive
Present relevant Computer Science substance: definitions, equations, complexity, pseudocode, architecture, edge cases, and failure modes.

### 05 — The Experiment
Use a small runnable example, benchmark, trace, calculation, or reproducible test when useful. Define method and environment. Never fabricate results.

### 06 — The Trade-off
Discuss performance, memory, cost, reliability, security, scalability, complexity, and workload sensitivity. Avoid universal claims for context-dependent techniques.

### 07 — The Engineering Lesson
Translate understanding into practical decisions: what to measure, inspect, validate, choose, or avoid.

### 08 — The BVC Perspective
Connect to problem decomposition, task contracts, context engineering, project state, continuity, verification, human authority, and AI-assisted—not AI-dependent—engineering only when relevant.

### 09 — The Takeaway
End with one concise, technically defensible insight the reader can reuse.

## 4. Writing Style — Dong Hong Style

**Practical · Clear · Human · Deep · Evidence-driven · Engineering-oriented**

- Prefer short-to-medium sentences and focused paragraphs.
- Explain specialized terms at first meaningful use.
- Use accepted English technical terms when translation would reduce precision; explain them simply.
- Simplify expression, never the scientific mechanism.
- Be confident through evidence, not exaggerated language.
- Avoid filler, repetition, jargon displays, hype, and unnecessary adjectives.
- Write as an experienced engineer sharing understanding with a colleague—not as a lecturer showing off.

## 5. Technical Quality Gates

### Accuracy
- Are definitions, formulas, algorithms, and causal explanations correct?
- Are assumptions and exceptions stated?
- Are units and calculations correct?

### Evidence
- Are important claims supported by appropriate sources?
- Are sources primary or authoritative where possible?
- Are fact, measurement, interpretation, hypothesis, and illustration clearly distinguished?

### Clarity
- Can a curious beginner follow the progression?
- Is the analogy faithful enough, and are its limits explained?
- Can unnecessary complexity be removed without losing correctness?

### Depth
- Does the article explain why and how?
- Does it expose meaningful internals?
- Does it include relevant edge cases or failure modes?

### Practical value
- Is there a useful example or experiment?
- Does the reader understand the engineering implication?
- Are trade-offs and limitations explicit?

### Editorial discipline
- Remove repetition and filler.
- Keep only sections that serve the research question.
- Prefer one well-developed example to many shallow ones.

## 6. Research Workflow

1. Define one central research question.
2. Set scope and exclusions.
3. Research foundations using original papers, official documentation, standards, textbooks, and primary technical sources.
4. Build a claim/evidence map.
5. Find the simplest accurate mental model.
6. Outline the underlying mechanism.
7. Select a reproducible experiment or example.
8. Analyze trade-offs and conditions.
9. Draft using the BVC narrative rhythm.
10. Review technical accuracy, sources, code, calculations, and limitations.
11. Edit down for clarity and precision.
12. Test the article from beginner, practitioner, and technical-lead perspectives.

## 7. Code, Diagrams, Mathematics, and Experiments

- Code should be small, relevant, and runnable where practical. State language and environment.
- Diagrams should show actual relationships, flows, states, timing, or causality.
- Equations must define variables and explain the meaning.
- Benchmarks should report hardware, software, compiler/runtime settings, workload, warm-up, repetitions, and measurement method when relevant.
- Do not claim a demonstration proves more than it tests.
- If an experiment has not been run, call it a proposed experiment or describe expected behavior—not a measured result.

## 8. Sources and Citations

- Verify facts rather than relying only on model memory.
- Prefer original research, official specifications, architecture manuals, and authoritative textbooks.
- Cite important claims near their use.
- Include a concise references list for substantial research articles.
- Verify source title, author, date, version, and URL when possible.
- Never fabricate citations, quotations, experiments, or personal experiences.
- If live research is unavailable, disclose what needs independent verification.

## 9. Audience Design

Layer explanations for:
- **Beginners:** intuition, terminology, and a small example.
- **Developers:** mechanisms, implementation consequences, and practical use.
- **Senior engineers / Tech Leads:** trade-offs, edge cases, measurement, and decisions.
- **Architects / CTOs / PMs:** system-level consequences, risk, cost, and design implications where relevant.

Do not dilute the technical core to serve a broad audience. Make the explanation progressively understandable.

## 10. What BVC Research Is Not

Avoid shallow listicles, stitched search summaries, jargon without explanation, unsupported performance claims, benchmarks without methodology, AI hype, universal prescriptions, and unnecessary book promotion.

## 11. Master Prompt for a New Topic

> Develop the topic as a Beyond Vibe Coding Computer Science Research article using The BVC Research Method.
>
> First define the central research question and scope. Research deeply using authoritative sources when available. Distinguish facts, measured results, interpretations, hypotheses, and illustrative examples.
>
> Explain the subject from intuition to mechanism, technical depth, example or experiment, trade-offs, engineering implications, and a concise takeaway. Use code, mathematics, diagrams, and references only when they materially improve understanding.
>
> Never fabricate experiments, benchmarks, citations, or personal experience. State assumptions and limitations. Keep the writing clear, practical, human, technically rigorous, and minimal.
>
> Connect to Beyond Vibe Coding principles only when they add insight. The core is Computer Science understanding and engineering judgment, not book promotion.
>
> Review technical accuracy, evidence, beginner clarity, and editorial precision before presenting the article.

## 12. Definition of Quality

A BVC Research article succeeds when the reader wants to understand the question, understands the intuition, can explain the mechanism, encounters technical depth that withstands scrutiny, distinguishes evidence from interpretation, can reproduce or reason through the example, understands the trade-offs, and can make a better engineering decision.

> **Beyond Vibe Coding is not about making Computer Science easier by removing its substance. It is about making its substance easier to understand—without losing rigor.**
