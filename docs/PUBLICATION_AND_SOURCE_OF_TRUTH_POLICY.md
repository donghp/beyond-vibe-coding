## 1. Official author and brand identity

The official author/brand identity for technology content is:

**Dong Hong · Beyond Vibe Coding**

Use this identity consistently in article footers, repository documentation, diagrams, technical infographics, social profiles, author biographies, and publication metadata.

## 2. Two official technology-content sources

Beyond Vibe Coding maintains two complementary official sources:

### 2.1 GitHub — original engineering repository and content source of truth

Repository: https://github.com/donghp/beyond-vibe-coding

GitHub is the canonical repository for original technology content and its supporting engineering materials, including:

- Original technical manuscripts and source Markdown.
- Research notes, evidence registers, and bibliographies.
- Code, examples, experiments, and reproducibility materials.
- Diagrams, image source assets, and figure descriptions.
- Revision history, correction records, metadata, and content governance.
- Series roadmaps and publication records.

GitHub is the durable source of truth for the repository version of the work. The repository commit history provides a public record of revisions; it is not, by itself, a legal registration or conclusive proof of authorship.

### 2.2 DEV.to — official English technology publication channel

Author profile: https://dev.to/donghp

DEV.to is the official English-language publication channel for the author's technology articles and technical essays. Articles published there should identify the author as **Dong Hong · Beyond Vibe Coding** and link to their supporting GitHub materials when relevant.

For an article with a repository manuscript, the GitHub file is the maintained source manuscript and DEV.to is the official reader-facing publication. If a DEV.to article includes platform-specific editing or updates, the publication record should identify the relationship clearly.

## 3. Community and social distribution channels

LinkedIn, Medium, X, Facebook, and other communities are distribution and audience-engagement channels.

They may contain:
- Short promotional posts linking to the official article.
- Summaries, excerpts, and commentary.
- Platform-adapted versions with different introductions or examples.
- Images and infographics that point back to the full article and repository source.

They are not automatically additional canonical originals.

### Canonical-link rules

- Exact republication: retain the original canonical URL where the platform supports canonical metadata.
- Substantial adaptation: identify the content as an adapted edition and link to the full original.
- Short social post: link directly to the official DEV.to article or the relevant GitHub source file.
- Do not present a copied community post as the first or exclusive source of the work.
- Avoid publishing near-identical full articles across multiple sites without clear canonical handling.

## 4. Recommended article metadata

Use a consistent author identity and a meaningful topic-specific title. For the GitHub manuscript, include the repository path in the publication record. For DEV.to, link the related source file in the article or supporting resources when practical.

Recommended author byline:

## 5. Copyright and attribution

Use this exact notice in Markdown files where a copyright notice is appropriate:

`Copyright © 2026 **Dong Hong · Beyond Vibe Coding**. All rights reserved.`

This notice identifies the author and brand. Third-party source material, software, quotations, diagrams, trademarks, and datasets remain subject to their respective owners and licenses. Always cite and verify reuse permissions.

Do not add the notice to third-party works as though the author owns them. Do not remove applicable third-party attribution or license notices.

## 6. Search visibility and discoverability

Repository content should be discoverable through clear information architecture and useful technical content rather than keyword repetition.

Recommended practices:
- Make the root README the clearest description of Beyond Vibe Coding and its technical scope.
- Use descriptive titles and first-paragraph summaries for important topic pages.
- Link topic pages, research indexes, practical examples, and published articles together.
- Use stable file names and meaningful headings.
- Add relevant author and source references to article pages.
- Keep content original, specific, accurate, and useful to readers.
- Use the same brand identity consistently across GitHub, DEV.to, and distribution profiles.
- Avoid keyword stuffing, artificial backlinks, duplicate pages, and misleading metadata.

Search engines decide crawling, indexing, canonical selection, and ranking. This policy does not guarantee indexing or any particular search position.

## 7. Publication record

Every series should maintain a publication register with:
- Article ID and title.
- Repository source path and revision.
- Official DEV.to URL and publication date.
- Adaptation/syndication URLs and canonical relationship.
- Update and correction history.

Only mark a publication as live when the public URL has been checked.
